## Format menu — pick ONE per article, and say why

| Format | When to use | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Number-led listicle/toolkit** — "7 automations that...", "5 things I refuse to..." | Tools, tactics, mistakes, roundups | Number-led titles get +47% reactions (34.3 vs 23.4) and only 1.6% of posts use them [94K] |
| **First-person receipts** — "How I did X" / "I tried X for 30 days" with real numbers | You have a real result, experiment, or build to show | First-person titles +30% reactions AND highest comment ratio (0.335) — massively underused at 5.5% of posts [94K] |
| **Contrarian take / myth-bust** — "You don't need X", "Stop doing Y", "Nobody needs another Z" | A belief your audience holds is wrong or expensive | Negative-framed titles get a +60% reaction premium (36.1 avg); only 5% of posts use it [94K] |
| **Teardown / behind-the-scenes** — pull apart a system, funnel, tool stack, or your own numbers | Showing how something actually works under the hood | Behind-the-scenes transparency is a top Notes/post converter [CARRIE]; utility beats commentary for conversion [W@W] |
| **Personal narrative with a business payoff** — story first, lesson lands late | Relationship and trust building; something actually happened to you | Story-based beats tip-based for conversion; "personal story + data" was the single best-converting format (514 subs in a month) [WES][CARRIE] |
| **Quick fix** — one problem, one fix, short | A small sharp problem your readers hit this week | High-efficiency: short posts earn the most reactions per word (4.8/100w vs 1.5) [94K]; "quick fix" is a proven converter format [CARRIE] |

Rules on picking:
- Default to **first-person receipts** or **contrarian** when the topic allows — both are underused and both outperform.
- A rant = contrarian format. A build/result = receipts. A "what tools" question = listicle. A story = personal narrative.
- Pure how-to guides still work for authority, but package them as a listicle or receipts post — "how to" phrasing in the title costs opens (see titles below).

## Titles + subtitles (it's an inbox subject AND a feed headline)

- **Length: aim 10-17 words.** 13-17-word titles average +27% reactions vs short ones; ceiling ~17 [94K]. BUT Gmail truncates ~70 chars, Yahoo ~46 [W@W] → **front-load the payoff in the first ~45 characters**; the back half is feed bonus. (Conflict noted: feed rewards long, inbox truncates — front-loading resolves it.)
- **Use "I/my" or "you/your"** — pronoun titles beat no-pronoun by ~30% [94K].
- **Numbers where honest** — +47% [94K].
- **Negative framing where honest** — "never/nobody/wrong/don't" +60% [94K].
- **Avoid "How to..." openers** — they signal effort; one tester found -57% opens [thrivewithcarrie.substack.com/p/newsletter-subject-lines-open-rates-curiosity-gap]. Reframe: "How to automate onboarding" → "I automated my onboarding in one afternoon (steal the setup)".
- Question marks: slight reaction drag (-6.7%) but the highest comment pull — use only when the question is genuinely provocative [94K].
- **Always write a subtitle, 6-10 words, adding NEW information** (not a restatement) — 6-10-word subtitles outperform none by ~20% [94K].
- Title formulas that map to the menu: "N [things] that [outcome]" · "I [did X] and [specific result]" · "You don't need [sacred cow] — you need [cheaper truth]" · "Inside my [system]: the [numbers] nobody shows you" · "The [time]-minute fix for [pain]".
- Substack A/B tests titles natively; in ~60% of tests a non-original title won — offer 2-3 title options per draft [SUB, on.substack.com/p/new-on-substack-ab-testing-for-headlines].

## Length + structure

- **Target 1,000-2,000 words (4-8 min read).** 2,000+ words wins raw reactions (35-37 avg vs 14-16 under 500) but costs the most to make; 1,000-2,000 is the defensible weekly zone [94K]. Quick-fix format may run 500-800 — deliberately, not lazily.
- **3-5 `## ` section headers** breaking the body (the house rule in `substack-structure.md`). Short paragraphs, one-line paragraphs for punch, bullets for recaps.
- **Always a cover image** — posts without one average 45% fewer reactions (12.7 vs 22.9) [94K].
- Don't sweat publish day — day-of-week effect is noise (all days share a median of 6 reactions) [94K].

## Openings (the first screen decides)

- Open with **an anecdote mid-action, a bold contrarian claim, or a shocking specific number** — the three hook types that keep people reading [substack.com/home/post/p-146305045; stlcc.edu writing centre].
- 1-4 short lines max before the frame. No greeting, no throat-clearing, no yes/no questions ("Have you ever...?" = shrug and close) [writeyournovel.substack.com].
- The opener must promise the payoff of the CHOSEN format: listicle → the sharpest item teased; receipts → the result up front; contrarian → the sacred cow named in line one.

## CTA + subscribe + paywall

- **End-of-post is the highest-converting position** — by the bottom, they've decided you're worth it [reallygoodbusinessideas.com/p/substack-buttons]. Keep the ONE-CTA rule.
- CTA copy carries a reason: "Want the next teardown in your inbox? Subscribe" beats a naked button [same source].
- Native subscribe button at the end — it adapts to the reader's status [SUB support docs].
- Don't stack mid-post CTAs in a 1,000-2,000-word article; one mid-post subscribe button only in 2,500+ posts.
- Paywall basics (relevant once you go paid): benchmark free→paid is 2-5%; keep ~3:1 free-to-paid content; if paywalling, put ~1,000 words of value above the wall; paid converts on utility (templates, playbooks) not more essays [W@W substack-paywalls-paid-subscribers-strategies; yana-g-y.com/p/substack-free-to-paid-conversion-rate].

## Companion Notes (the growth engine — every article ships with 1-2 Notes)

Why: Substack's own numbers — the app/network drives **50% of free and 30% of paid subscriptions** [SUB, on.substack.com/p/how-publishers-are-using-notes-to-grow]; ~40% of new subscriptions now come from inside the network [escapethecubicle.substack.com/p/substack-just-revealed-40-of-new]. One creator tracked **62% of her last 500 subscribers to Notes vs 4% to posts** [CARRIE].

Note formats that convert to SUBSCRIBERS (not just likes) [CARRIE][WES]:
1. **Personal story + data** — a real moment tied to a specific number/result (best converter)
2. **Contrarian take** — challenge a belief the niche holds
3. **Quick fix** — one sharp problem, one fix, in the note itself
4. **Vulnerable confession** — a failure or fear, told straight
5. **Behind-the-scenes numbers** — revenue/results transparency
6. **Conversation starter** — a genuine question

Rules for companion notes:
- Draft **2 notes per article**: one STANDALONE-VALUE note (story/contrarian/quick-fix carrying the article's sharpest idea — the note must be worth reading if they never click) + one soft **"new post" teaser** with the link. 80/20 value-to-promo across the account [CARRIE].
- **Stories over tips** — convert any tip note into "here's what happened when I..." [WES].
- Notes are plain text, first line is the hook, short lines, no hashtags, no markdown.
- Engagement ≠ conversion: a viral note that doesn't sound like the newsletter converts nothing — keep notes on your 3-4 content pillars [WES].
- Frequency (your call, not the skill's): algorithm creator says ~1 original note/day is the sweet spot [ALGO]; growth-maxxers run 3-5+/day [CARRIE]. Conflict noted — consistency beats volume [ALGO].
- **Restack your own post the morning after publishing** (+20-30% views per post) [RESTACK]. Suggest it in the report.
- Distribution is audience-overlap driven: notes reach followers of similar writers, so niche consistency IS the targeting [ALGO].
- 3+ notes in launch/publish week correlates with +50% subscriber gain for new pubs [SUB].

## Cadence + network effects

- **Weekly is the benchmark** (Substack's data team, from thousands of publications) [SUB, on.substack.com/p/substack-writing-consistently]; bestseller-tier writers average 2-4.4 posts/week [finntropy.substack.com/p/how-often-should-you-write-to-grow]. Predictable beats frequent.
- Recommendations from other publications are a top growth source — being recommendable (clear niche, consistent quality) matters more than gaming anything [SUB].
- Comments on other Substacks in the niche = free qualified visibility [buildtolaunch.substack.com/p/how-to-grow-substack-from-zero-in-2026].

## What gets punished or ignored

- **AI-sounding prose is a trust killer**: reader trust drops ~50% the moment they suspect AI [AI-TELL, Raptive study]; human-sounding content pulls ~2x the engagement of AI slop [SUB AI report]. Readers now pattern-match on: em-dash clusters, "not X but Y" constructions, neat lists of three, symmetrical rhythm. Natural em-dashes are fine — but the language gate is the floor, and CLUSTERS of these patterns are the tell.
- Corporate content-marketing tone (funnel-speak, multi-offer stacks, "value-packed") — reads as marketing, not writing. Substack is article-first.
- Over-linking out: every external link is an exit; link to your own posts or site only where it serves the reader (the links block stays at 3 or fewer).
- Clickbait that under-delivers: Substack surfaces what gets READ and restacked, not just opened — the body has to cash the title's cheque [ALGO][SUB].
- Vanity-metric chasing on Notes: high-like notes off-pillar bring zero subscribers [WES].

## Conflicts in the research (be honest about these)

1. **Length**: 94K-post data says longer wins raw reactions; several coaches say 500-1,200; Sarah Fay [W@W] says there's no universal answer. Resolution: 1,000-2,000 default, format decides.
2. **Notes volume**: 1/day (algorithm creator) vs 3-7/day (growth accounts). Resolution: quality + consistency first; this skill ships 2 per article and leaves daily cadence to you.
3. **Titles**: feed rewards 13-17 words; inboxes truncate at ~46-70 chars. Resolution: front-load, and use Substack's native A/B test.
4. **Questions in titles**: fewer reactions but more comments — use for discussion posts only.
