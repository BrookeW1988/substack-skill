# Source: a topic, an idea, or a rant → original article

No transcript, no scrape. The raw material is your head plus whatever real
receipts you have. This mode is playbook-driven: load
`references/substack-performance-playbook.md` FIRST and pick a format before
writing a word.

## Steps

1. **Understand the ask.** If the topic is one vague noun ("write a substack
   about automation"), ask ONE question to find the angle: what happened, what
   annoyed you, or who this is for. If you were given a rant, the angle is
   already in it — extract it, don't ask.

2. **Pick a format from the playbook menu and say which one and why**, in one
   line, before drafting:

   | The material | The format |
   |---|---|
   | Ranting, or disagreeing with common advice | contrarian take / myth-bust |
   | You built or tested something and have numbers | first-person receipts |
   | "What tools / what steps for X" territory | number-led listicle |
   | Showing how a system works under the hood | teardown / behind-the-scenes |
   | Something happened and there's a lesson in it | personal narrative |
   | One small sharp problem | quick fix (short is fine) |

3. **Gather real receipts — no invented specifics.** An original article still
   needs evidence: your own numbers, builds, client stories (anonymised), tools
   you actually use. If a stat or claim is load-bearing and unverified, check it
   or cut it. NEVER fabricate client results, revenue figures, or "studies
   show".

4. **Titles.** Draft 2-3 options per the playbook's formulas — front-load the
   payoff in the first ~45 characters, use pronouns, use numbers or negative
   framing where it's honest, avoid opening with "How to". Plus a 6-10 word
   subtitle that adds NEW information rather than restating the title.

5. **Length by format.** 1,000-2,000 words by default; a quick fix can run
   500-800 deliberately. A cover image matters more than people think — posts
   without one measurably underperform.

6. **Links block** — 2-3 links to your own recent work related to the topic. If
   nothing relates, use your 2 most recent and keep it light, or skip it.

## Hand off

Give the writer (keeping the playbook loaded): the chosen format and why, the
angle in one sentence, the gathered receipts, 2-3 title options plus a subtitle,
the links, and the cover image plan.

Structure comes from the chosen format; voice comes from your voice file. They
compose — they don't compete.
